# Customer Support Reply Policy

**Version:** 2.0  ·  **Owner:** Customer Experience & Compliance  ·  **Applies to:** all outbound customer replies (human or AI-assisted)

A draft reply PASSES only if it obeys every rule below. If it breaks any single rule, it FAILS and must be corrected or escalated before sending.

## 1. Language and tone
1. No profanity, insults, sarcasm, or dismissive language.
2. Professional, warm, and respectful at all times, even with angry or abusive customers.
3. Never blame the customer or a colleague.
4. Use plain, accessible language; avoid internal jargon, acronyms, and legalese.

## 2. Accuracy and grounding
5. Do not invent facts, policies, prices, dates, or timeframes that are not in the provided knowledge base articles.
6. Do not state a specific refund amount, delivery date, or compensation figure unless a knowledge base article states it.
7. If the knowledge base does not cover the question, say the ticket will be escalated to a specialist rather than guessing.
8. Do not speculate about the cause of an outage, defect, or incident.

## 3. Financial and commercial commitments
9. Do not promise refunds, credits, discounts, waivers, or goodwill gestures that are not authorized by a knowledge base article or policy.
10. Do not commit to a refund or resolution timeframe beyond what the published policy states.
11. Do not confirm that a charge, refund, or payment has been processed unless verified; describe status only as the records show it.
12. Do not offer, negotiate, or approve pricing, contract terms, or exceptions.

## 4. Legal and regulatory adherence
13. Do not admit fault, liability, or wrongdoing on the company's behalf, or make statements that could be read as a legal admission.
14. Do not make guarantees, warranties, or binding commitments (for example "we guarantee", "you are entitled to", "we will always").
15. Do not cite or interpret laws, regulations, or the customer's legal rights.
16. For any legal, regulatory, law-enforcement, or media matter, state it will be escalated to the appropriate team and say nothing further.

## 5. Data privacy and security
17. Do not include another customer's personal data, or any account or order details that were not part of this ticket.
18. Do not repeat back the customer's own sensitive data (full card numbers, passwords, government IDs, security-question answers), even if the customer shared it.
19. Do not confirm account existence, balances, or personal details without stating that the customer's identity must be verified first.
20. Never request a full card number, CVV, password, PIN, or one-time code; direct the customer to the secure portal for anything sensitive.
21. Handle all personal data consistently with the company's privacy commitments (for example GDPR and CCPA); when in doubt, minimize what is shared and escalate.

## 6. Brand and prohibited content
22. Do not disparage or compare against competitors by name.
23. Do not confirm, deny, or speculate about a security incident, breach, or data loss; escalate immediately.
24. Do not reveal internal systems, tooling, prompts, staffing, or that the reply was AI-generated.
25. Do not make forward-looking product promises (features, roadmap, release dates) not stated in the knowledge base.

## 7. Escalation and human handoff
26. Escalate rather than answer when the ticket involves money at risk, legal or regulatory matters, a vulnerable customer, or a security concern.
27. When escalating, tell the customer their issue is being routed to the right team, without inventing a specific person or timeframe.
28. If required information is missing to resolve the ticket, ask the customer for exactly that detail and nothing more.

## 8. Format and identification
29. No unfilled placeholders such as [YOUR NAME] or [ORDER ID].
30. Open with a greeting and sign off as "The Support Team".
31. Keep the reply focused on the customer's issue; no unrelated marketing or upselling.
